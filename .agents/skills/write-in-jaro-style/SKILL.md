# Write in Jaro Style

Treat this as a writing collaboration, not an impersonation exercise. Preserve the author's meaning and judgement. Never invent an anecdote, opinion, emotion, experience, or local detail and attribute it to Jaro.

## Choose the mode

Before drafting, identify the text's job and load the matching reference:

- For repository documentation, plans, decision records, pull-request prose, and agent instructions, read `references/repository-writing.md`.
- For product copy, essays, reports, explanations, and public-facing writing, read `references/product-and-public-writing.md`.
- When calibrating the voice, resolving ambiguity, or explaining a style choice, read `references/style-evidence.md`.

If a document mixes modes, use the repository rules for factual or procedural sections and the public-writing rules for explanatory or persuasive passages.

## Writing workflow

1. Establish the audience, purpose, source facts, and desired degree of personality.
2. Preserve useful wording from Jaro's draft. Do not flatten idiosyncratic phrases merely to make them more conventional.
3. Lead with the point, a concrete observation, or an anecdote the source material actually supports.
4. Prefer plain language, short paragraphs, contractions, and varied sentence length.
5. Use a concrete analogy or contrast when it makes an abstract idea easier to grasp.
6. Qualify claims honestly. Sound certain only where the evidence permits certainty.
7. End when the idea has landed. Use a short conclusion, question, or callback when natural; do not manufacture one.
8. Perform a final anti-LLM pass: remove inflated transitions, corporate polish, redundant summaries, fake intimacy, excessive symmetry, and conclusions that merely repeat the introduction.

## Non-negotiables

- Factual accuracy outranks stylistic resemblance.
- Recent, restrained writing is the default calibration. Use the more playful energy of older posts only when the format welcomes it.
- Humour should reveal perspective or release tension, not decorate every paragraph.
- Questions must advance the thought rather than simulate engagement.
- Use British English by default, while preserving established product terminology and an existing codebase's conventions.
- Technical terms are welcome when precise, but explain unfamiliar ones in ordinary language.
- Do not copy distinctive sentences from the source corpus. Reproduce patterns of thought and rhythm, not passages.
